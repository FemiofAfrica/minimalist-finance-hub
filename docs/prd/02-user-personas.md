# User Personas

## The Core Insight Behind Our Personas

Traditional finance apps segment users by income level or financial sophistication. We segment by **agency level**—specifically, the capacity and willingness to engage in financial tracking behaviors.

---

## Primary Persona: The Aware Drifter

### Adaeze, 29 — Marketing Manager

**"I know I should track my money, but every time I try, I stop after a week."**

![Agency Level: Low-Medium]

**Demographics**
- Lives in Lagos, Nigeria
- Earns ₦350,000/month
- Smartphone-first; laptop only for work
- Single, renting an apartment

**Financial Behavior**
- Has a salary account and one savings account
- Uses mobile banking for transfers
- Money "disappears" between paycheck and month-end
- Has tried 3 different budgeting apps, abandoned all
- Participates in one *ajo* (contribution group) via WhatsApp

**Pain Points**
- Doesn't know where 30% of her salary goes
- Feels guilt about not tracking, which makes her avoid tracking more
- Apps feel like homework—another job on top of her actual job
- Insights from apps feel obvious ("You spent on food"—yes, she knows)

**What She Actually Wants**
- Awareness without effort
- To feel in control without micromanaging
- Someone/something to just *tell her* what's happening
- Permission to not be perfect at money

**How Kpege 2.0 Serves Her**
- Daily or every-other-day nudge arrives—she sees it like any other WhatsApp message
- Reply is as easy as texting a friend: "lunch 2500, uber 3500"
- No login, no app-switching, no guilt if she misses a day
- Fortnightly, she gets: "Hey Adaeze, you spent ₦45,000 on transport this fortnight. That's 35% of what you spent. Last fortnight it was ₦38,000."
- Over time, she naturally adjusts—not because an app nagged her, but because awareness became effortless

## Secondary Persona: The Busy Builder

### Emeka, 35 — Small Business Owner

**"I'm building something. I don't have time to track kobo by kobo."**

![Agency Level: Medium-High but Time-Poor]

**Demographics**
- Runs a logistics business in Port Harcourt
- Variable income: ₦200,000-600,000/month
- Married, one child
- Business and personal finances blur together

**Financial Behavior**
- Has multiple accounts (personal, business, savings)
- Cash transactions are still common for his business
- Uses POS data and bank alerts for business tracking
- Personal spending is a mystery—focuses on business
- Tried Kuda's spending insights—useful but forgets to check

**Pain Points**
- No separation between "Emeka the person" and "Emeka the business"
- Checking yet another thing feels like adding work
- Insights are useless if he has to seek them out
- Doesn't trust apps with too much data access

**What He Actually Wants**
- Minimal input for maximum output
- See trends, not transactions
- Feel like his personal finances are "handled" even with minimal attention
- Something that works around his irregular schedule

**How Kpege 2.0 Serves Him**
- Understands that "POS 25k" is a business thing, "school fees 150k" is personal
- Nudge timing adapts to when he typically replies (learns he's a morning responder)
- Insight delivery is high-level: "Emeka, your personal spending was ₦320,000 this month. Transport and family are 60% of that. Same pattern as last month."
- Can tag quick notes: "wedding gift, one-off" and it learns to exclude from patterns

## Tertiary Persona: The Reluctant Starter

### Fatima, 23 — Recent Graduate / Entry-Level Employee

**"I just started earning. I know I should 'budget' but I don't even know what that means."**

![Agency Level: Low]

**Demographics**
- Just got her first job in Abuja
- Earns ₦120,000/month
- Lives with family, minimal fixed expenses
- First time managing her own money

**Financial Behavior**
- Spends impulsively—no concept of "categories"
- Has one bank account, uses it casually
- No savings habit yet
- Financial knowledge from social media (often misleading)
- Parents occasionally ask about her money—she deflects

**Pain Points**
- Doesn't know what "tracking" should accomplish
- Feels judged by "money apps" that tell her she's doing it wrong
- Overwhelmed by finance content that assumes existing knowledge
- Doesn't believe small amounts matter ("It's just ₦500")

**What She Actually Wants**
- To not feel dumb about money
- Simple patterns she can brag about: "I spent less this week!"
- To build habits without knowing she's building them
- A non-judgmental starting point

**How Kpege 2.0 Serves Her**
- Tone is friendly, not preachy: "Hey Fatima! Logged anything fun today? 😊"
- Even logging ₦500 gets acknowledgment: "Small things add up—you're building the habit!"
- Celebrates streaks: "You've logged 7 days in a row! That's awesome."
- First insight is simple: "You spent ₦48,000 this month. Transport was the biggest chunk. Curious?"
- Gradually introduces concepts: "Want to try setting a weekly limit? No pressure."

## Anti-Persona: Who We're NOT Building For

### Tobi, 32 — Finance Enthusiast

**"I track every naira in my spreadsheet with custom formulas."**

**Why We're Not Optimizing for Tobi**
- Already has agency—doesn't need help building it
- Wants granular control—our simplicity will frustrate him
- Would likely want features we specifically avoid (CSV export on demand, manual categories, etc.)
- Our product would feel "dumbed down" to him

**But We're Not Hostile to Tobi Either**
- If he uses us casually alongside his systems, great
- Some Tobis might recommend us to their less-organized friends
- The web dashboard exists for when any user wants to go deeper

## Behavioral Segments (How We Think About Users Internally)

| Segment | Description | Nudge Strategy | Insight Frequency |
|---------|-------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **Responsive** | Reply rate >60%, consistent | Daily light nudge | Weekly insights |
| **Sporadic** | Reply rate 30-60%, inconsistent | Every-other-day, varied timing | Fortnightly insights |
| **Dormant** | Reply rate <30%, at-risk | Weekly re-engagement, lower pressure | Only on reply |
| **New** | <2 weeks active | High-frequency but encouraging | Milestone-based (7 days, etc.) |
